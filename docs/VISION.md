# The long vision

**Recorded:** 2026-08-05
**Source:** Phil, in conversation, unprompted and in one pass
**Status:** Primary source. Not a company vision statement — the thing underneath one.

---

## In Phil's words

Preserved close to verbatim. Only obvious typos corrected. The rhythm is his and it matters.

> My vision is a world where humans understand their place.
>
> Imagine this. Domed cities, where all air is filtered before it leaves. Pure, clean air,
> devoid of human interaction apart from the filter. Domed cities where people live within
> their means, but they do not want for anything, because they can get everything they need.
> They get this by understanding their place in the world, and sticking to it. In the cities,
> there is art, entertainment, purpose, happiness, health, and education. For all. The cities
> are clean, no-one wants to be dirty, as they see there's no need to be dirty, they don't
> have to be. Enough food is grown for everyone. Money doesn't come into it. There's no need
> for money. People just do what needs to be done to keep the city rolling.
>
> Then, outside the domes, nature is left to go for it. And nature will go for it. But there
> is always work to do. And the cities need their resources. So, all mining is undertaken in
> the most clean and environmentally safe way possible. Not profitable, just what is needed.
> Water is taken, used, filtered, and put back. Air is taken, used, filtered, and put back.
>
> Humans in suits with helmets that have AI built in worked the outside world. Always
> connected, always on a mission. The earth needs help, and these humans who work outside the
> city are on a mission. Clean up the residue we left behind before the migration to the
> domes. The poisons, the plants in the wrong place, the landfills, the plastics. Cleaned up,
> every day, by people on a mission.

## What this is

This is not a vision statement for a web and automation business, and it should never be
presented as one. It is a picture of what humans getting it right looks like, held by the
person who runs the business. It is the layer beneath the company — the reason the company
is worth running rather than the description of what it does.

Every organisation has one of these. Almost none write it down, which is why their stated
values read like they were assembled from a list.

Note also *how* it is written. Flat declaratives, repetition used as structure, no adjectives
doing sales work, a refrain that returns — *on a mission*, *taken, used, filtered, and put
back*. That is exactly the droning prose described in §7.3 of the brand spec, produced without
trying. Phil's unperformed voice is already the brand voice. When copy later sounds wrong,
this passage is the reference for what right sounds like.

## What's in it

Eight things, extracted. These are the parts that carry downward into the business.

| Principle | In the vision | What it means below |
|---|---|---|
| **Place, not dominance** | Humans understand their place | The organising idea. Not stewardship-as-control; knowing what is yours to do |
| **Sufficiency** | Live within their means, want for nothing | Enough is a real quantity and it is reachable. Not scarcity, not consumption |
| **No rent** | Money doesn't come into it | Nothing charges you again for what you already have |
| **The city runs** | People just do what needs to be done to keep the city rolling | The city is a drone. It operates by operating. Maintenance, not supervision |
| **The boundary is real** | The dome wall | There is an inside and an outside, and they are different places |
| **Outside is where the work is** | Nature left to go for it; there is always work to do | Value is created outside the boundary, not within the comfortable interior |
| **Closed loops** | Taken, used, filtered, and put back | Nothing is consumed and abandoned. What you take, you return |
| **Cleanup** | The poisons, the plants in the wrong place, the plastics | The job is largely undoing damage already done, not building new things |

## Why this is already in the brand

None of the following was designed. It was arrived at twice, separately, and the pieces fit
because they came from the same person.

**The dome wall is the boundary.** The brand spec makes *silver* the boundary colour and gives
it one job, so the boundary is always visible — "which matters, because the brand is about
being outside it" (§5.3). The vision has a literal wall in it, with the comfortable conforming
interior on one side and the real work on the other. *Rogue*, in the 1560s legal sense the
spec adopts, means masterless — no parish, no place in the order. The suited workers outside
the dome are rogue by that definition.

**The city is the drone.** §2.1 defines a drone as anything that operates by just operating —
"it works whether or not anyone is watching, and it does not need to think in order to work."
The city needs no money, no management and no supervision. People do what needs doing and it
keeps rolling. That is the definition, at civilisational scale.

**The suited worker is the kārearea.** §2.4: the bird "registers where something needs doing,
acts where it needs to act, leaves a drone behind, and moves on. Attention is outward, on the
terrain — never inward on the machinery." The vision's workers are outside, always connected,
attention on the terrain, AI built into the helmet. Same figure. And the same working method
Phil already uses — he is not a developer; he is a person with AI in the helmet who can read
a situation.

**Taken, used, filtered, and put back is the anti-rent position.** The strongest emotion in
any of Phil's three founding stories was anger at Squarespace charging an artist $45 a month
for a site she built herself and would lose the moment she stopped paying. That is extraction
without return. The vision's water cycle is its exact opposite, and it is why "no per-seat
fee, no renewal you forgot about" (§7.3) is not a pricing tactic but a position.

**Always on a mission.** Phil used the word before the word had been defined.

## What does not transfer

Recorded honestly, so nobody later mistakes this document for a set of instructions.

1. **Money.** The vision has none. Rogue Drones must charge, and charge enough to survive, or
   it helps nobody. The transferable part is not the absence of money but the absence of
   *rent* — pay for the work, own the result, never pay again for what you already have.

2. **"Understanding their place in the world, and sticking to it."** Read in context this is
   plainly ecological — humans knowing what is theirs to take, in a world where art,
   education, health and purpose are available to everyone. Read cold by a stranger, the same
   words can be made to sound like something considerably uglier about people staying in
   their social station. This is the same category of problem as the private shorthand Phil
   uses for his own compulsion to optimise: true, useful, and unusable in public in these
   words. The idea keeps. The phrasing needs replacing before it goes anywhere near a client.

3. **It cannot be failed.** A vision of domed cities is not a thing anyone can hold Rogue
   Drones to, which is fine — that is what visions are for. It sets direction. The mission is
   the falsifiable part and it does not exist yet.

## What Rogue Drones does about it

The bridge, stated plainly and deliberately small.

The vision is a world where humans understand their place. Rogue Drones cannot build domed
cities. What it can do is the same operation at the only scale available to it: **take the
work that is holding a person at the wrong level, build something that does it without them,
and leave them free to do the part only they can do.**

Anja was on the ground placing traps when the organisation needed her thinking about where
traps go. The GorseBusters managers are inside a spreadsheet when they should be handing over
an organisation. Steph is administering a website when she should be making work. In every
case a person is doing something below their place, and a drone is what gets them back up to
it.

That is the vision's central claim — humans understanding their place — running at the scale
of one small organisation at a time. It is also, uncomfortably, the pattern already written up
in `F500 enterprises discovering AI-legible frameworks.md` under a completely different name:
offload the lower work so the higher level can climb, and the payoff exists only if the climb
actually happens.

Three arrivals at one idea, from a falcon, from a consulting thesis, and from a domed city.
